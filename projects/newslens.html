Why I built it

People increasingly get news from YouTube videos, where claims are hard to verify against written reporting. I wanted to study how the format of an AI-generated summary changes how carefully readers check what a video says.

What I built

A Gradio web app: enter a topic keyword and a YouTube URL, and it produces two versions of an analysis report.

  • News crawler — scrapes recent headlines and article text from Taiwanese news sites that match the keyword.
  • Timestamped transcription — downloads the video audio with yt-dlp and transcribes it with Whisper (via Groq).
  • Mode A · Minimal summary — conclusions only, simulating a low-effort reading context.
  • Mode B · Timeline verification — the same analysis with VTT timestamps and transcript quotes for cross-checking.

Technical highlights

  • Two-condition output by design — one pipeline generates both A and B reports from the same inputs, so they can serve as experiment stimuli.
  • Fast inference — Groq-hosted whisper-large-v3 and llama-3.1-8b-instant keep end-to-end latency low.
  • Persistence — crawled articles are stored in MongoDB for later inspection.

What I learned

Building the tool forced me to think about the reader as much as the model: the same facts presented with or without evidence invite very different levels of trust. Site-specific scrapers are also fragile, so a future version should rely on more stable news sources.
