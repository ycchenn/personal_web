Why I built it

My mother teaches elementary school, and her class ran on a paper tally of merit points. The recurring problem was "merit disputes": students contested their scores, and a paper tally offered no record of who earned which point, when, or why.

I treated her as the primary stakeholder — interviewing her to define requirements — and built a system where every point change leaves evidence.

What I built

A mobile-first web app a teacher can operate while walking around a busy classroom.

  • Real-time leaderboard — Firestore onSnapshot listeners update the board with no refresh.
  • Batch point adjustments — award or deduct points for several students in one action.
  • Weekly fresh start — the view resets each week while the full-semester record stays intact for final grading.
  • Rollback — undo a mistaken entry and have the balance corrected automatically.

Technical highlights

  • Atomic writes — log deletion and score correction run in a single Firestore writeBatch, so a balance can never drift from its history.
  • Temporal filtering instead of deletion — the "weekly reset" is a query filter on timestamps, not a wipe, which keeps an audit trail.
  • Composite indexes — custom indexes support sorting by points (desc) then seat number (asc).
  • Zero-server deployment — Firebase as BaaS plus GitHub Pages hosting.

Results

1,195point records logged over one semester
1paper accepted at TANET 2026

The system was used in a real classroom for a full semester and became the case study for my TANET 2026 paper on audit-trail-oriented classroom scoring.

What I learned

A system doesn't have to be complex to be valuable — it has to fit the user's actual day. The rollback and weekly-reset features both came from my mother's feedback after the first weeks of use, which taught me to ship early and let real usage decide what comes next.
