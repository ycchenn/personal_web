Why I built it

When an AI scores each arriving emergency patient's severity, the hospital has to decide which scores a human should double-check. Reviewing more patients catches AI mistakes on truly critical cases — but it uses the same staff time needed to treat everyone else.

What I built

An operations-research study built around a discrete-event simulation of an emergency department.

  • Simulation core — SimPy model with time-varying Poisson arrivals, a noisy AI severity score with confidence, and a shared staff pool for review and treatment.
  • Experiments — 40 replications per policy, a threshold sweep that traces the safety–workload trade-off, and sensitivity analysis on AI accuracy.
  • Interactive dashboard — a Streamlit app to adjust AI accuracy, staffing, and threshold and see results update live.
PolicyRule
No reviewAlways trust the AI score
Review allEvery patient gets a manual review
Fixed τReview when a risk score exceeds a constant threshold
Dynamic heuristicSame rule, but the threshold adapts to real-time staff congestion

Technical highlights

  • Boundary-weighted risk score — (1 − confidence) × w(ai_severity), where w up-weights scores near the critical / non-critical boundary, where missed emergencies hide.
  • Congestion-aware threshold — reviews more thoroughly when staff are idle and more selectively when busy.
  • Reproducible — synthetic data, tests, and a single script that regenerates every figure.

Results

≈0harmful misses, matching the safe fixed threshold
~31%less reviewer time
~27%shorter average patient wait

Across 40 simulated 12-hour shifts, the dynamic heuristic kept safety level with a conservative fixed threshold while concentrating review effort in quiet periods.

What I learned

Human–AI collaboration is a resource-allocation problem as much as a modelling one: the best policy isn't "trust the AI" or "check everything," but deciding when human attention is worth spending. All data here is synthetic, so the next step would be calibrating against real ED arrival patterns.
